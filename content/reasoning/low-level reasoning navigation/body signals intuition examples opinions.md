- **other notes (provisional description)**:
	 I'll describe each of the types of example here before I turn those into notes:

	- high-intensity training: this is right for my temperament. I was wrong to generalize it to other people but right that it was best for me. Reducing intensity so I (/Gie City) could do more sets is one of the biggest mistakes I've ever made - it turned training into an absolute grind because the volume was still too hard to get in (and this was still less than 10 sets per muscle group per week). And really held back my progress. Now I just do one top set once or twice a week, feel great, and am making good gains - the only barrier to gaining is more at the moment is the need for a "marinading phase" after making strength gains.
	- post-workout hunger: ... . Not eating during this period can't be made up for by eating more later - it's just throwing away the effort from the workout.

	- pain/damage: amount of pain seems to correspond to the amount of damage, but it's really a cost/benefit assessment of situation with damage already factored in.
	- injury recovery undertraining: it can seem like the right thing to do is avoid any activity that causes slight pain, but it's really just a matter of "progressive overload" (progressing load for constant overload). As long is pain is only mild. Injuries won't just recover by themselves - the only cases where they do is where "by themselves" is actually using the area as much as I can (without significant discomfort) without thinking about the fact that I'm doing it.
	- injury recovery careful training: sometimes it seems like the right thing to do is being "careful" while lifting the weight during the set, but it's really "choose the weight carefully, use the weight confidently"
