- **provisional description**:
	- ...
	- ... the idea was to use 2 single adjustable height cable machines and adjust the width between them to replicate a dual arm functional trainer. Dual arm functional trainers were too expensive at ~$5000 new in 2018. Gie City hadn't thought of the idea of looking for a second hand one or there were none on the market (I can't remember which of those right now).
	- The idea behind an adjustable height & width dual cable set-up was resistance curve - Gie City could have the cable cables coming at his body from any angle to have constant resistance through full RoM for every possible movement - all in one basic set-up.
	- To get that with free weights, you'd have to use one arm/leg at a time, tilt to the side for most exercises, and hang upside down or at steep decline angles while also tilting to the side for some exercises. At the time Gie City assumed that bilateral was better - it meant less sets to do - and he hadn't experienced the increase in agility that I've noticed now in 2026 from unilateral work. Agility wasn't one of his main goals back then anyway.
	- ...
	- So in summary, Gie City was wary of spending too much time trying to figure out free weight constant resistant set-ups. He thought having an adjustable height & width cable set-up up would a final, all-in-one solution to all resistance curve set-ups.
	- Gie City chose Bodyworx because it had cable machines all the other pieces from the same line - models matching visually (for visual aesthetics) and in branding (for conceptual aesthetics) was one of the main considerations after resistance curve/versatility.
	- ...
	- The cable machine idea turned out to be a disaster
	- Gie City hadn't planned a way of adjusting the width. He found them too heavy to drag between exercises. So first he spent a huge amount of time putting wheels on them. He put stacks of weight plates in front of them to stop them rolling. This actually worked pretty well apart from the set-up time of putting the weights in place.
	- But then as soon as he used it, he found that the weight stacks had way too much friction. I can't remember when it was that he solved the width adjustment problem and ran into the friction problem, but it might have been at some time in 2019. He messed around trying to reduce friction in the stacks until he had to get rid of the machines in 2021.
	- In 2021, my family had to move to a house with less space. So Gie City ended up selling all the Bodyworx stuff and getting Ironmaster stuff. At the time the cable machines weren't functioning and had been modified to the point were Gie City couldn't put them back together into the original design. There also wasn't much time to sell the Bodyworx stuff before moving - maybe a month or 2 - the switch to Ironmaster was a last minute idea. So without much time to wait for buyers, the rest of the Bodyworx equipment was sold for maybe 1/3 or 1/4 of the new price.
	- As for the cable machines - they couldn't be sold - so they were put in a skip. $2500 AUD spent in 2018, 3 years messing around with adjustment and friction reduction solutions, zero use out of them, and no resale money.
	- All the other pieces - the benches etc - worked well though. ...
