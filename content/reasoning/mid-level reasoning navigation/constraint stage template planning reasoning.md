- **provisional description**:
	- Reasoning about what to do for certain decisions, both questions and opinions
	- Part of "template planning reasoning" - the planning that applies to most situations, to build on with specific reasoning in "timespan planning reasoning"
	- Rationale for calling it "constraint stage " instead of "decision stage": people often talk about "decisions within constraints". But when they're choosing those initial boundaries for the options, they're *making a decision*. Those constraints are decisions. "Making decisions within constraints" is just "making decisions after making decisions". So where does the apparent difference between constraints and decisions in that phrase come from? I think it might be the fact that it's happening in organizations not personal lives. In organizations you have multiple teams all working towards deciding one overall course of action. Different teams work on different parts of the process of narrowing down the options. So one team might make a decision then another team has to decide what to do within those options. So the decision of one team becomes a constraint in the decision of others. This somewhat makes sense in an organization but I think my framing leads to a more holistic view of life planning. ...
