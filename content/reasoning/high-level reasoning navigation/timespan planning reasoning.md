- provisional description:
	- Planning for specific or unusual situations
	- E.g. planning for what to do if I lose my Disability Support Pension income. Or how to avoid or counteract antipsychotics. 
	- The point of this as a separate category is that my planning isn't just the conclusions. It's an argument map of the rationale and the conclusions. So if I was doing that for every situation individually, the I'd have hundreds of argument maps that were all mostly the same. For any given decision most of the rationale is core beliefs/higher-level life decisions that apply to most decisions. Maybe 80% of it would be overlap. So the solution to this is to have the reasoning that applies to *most* situations, then reasoning that goes *from those conclusions* to the conclusion about the situation. The general reasoning is called template reasoning. "Timespan reasoning" is the second part.
