  - other_notes (provisional_description): notes from when was first stage of reasoning in FIRST framework (first-principles planning/decsion framework)
  	- There are multiple criteria in the fundamental stage - the answer will be a set of considerations, not a single one. But you ask how to increase considerations *one at a time* in the instrumental stage. The "concise_descriptions" (and the "what-how-but-so name") don't really convey that - they can make it seem like there's only one fundamental consideration to find, or that you should try to think of solutions that maximize all criteria in one step. But this is probably as good as it gets for a mnemonic.
  	- Edit 2026/02/26:
  		- This used to be in main reasoning as the the first stage of planning in the FIRST framework. Now it's seen as a separate type of reasoning.
  		- The planning reasoning process now starts from which things (states of the world) would immediately positively affect your intrinsic values (based on what's your personal model stage opinions). The you ask how to bring about those states. Then when you notice that one of the actions compromises another value, you note that in the side effect stage. Then once you know all of those, you make the decisions about what's best overall in the trade-off stage. There's no reasoning about what to aim at a the start. Values are seen as "given" at the start, and reasoning about values is seen as as fundamentally different.
  		- I've put it in 2nd-order reasoning for now because it's "reasoning about what to aim at in your main reasoning", but it seems different from the other 2nd order reasoning ("how to go about the 1st order reasoning more effectively). I'll find out exactly where it fits in later.
