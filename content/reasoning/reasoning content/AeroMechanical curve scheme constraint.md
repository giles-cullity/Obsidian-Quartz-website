- **provisional description**:
	- All of the lines of the things I own need to conform to a certain "scheme" analogous to a color scheme**. 
	- I'd say that a color scheme is mainly about the set of colors allowed. The question of which parts of an object are which of those colors is separate. It's the same with my idea of a curve scheme. This says which types of curvature my possessions can have. The question of which parts have which of those is a further decision.
	- ...
	- "Aero"
		- "Aero" is my name for a certain type of curvature that's like some aerodynamic bodywork. It's half way between geometric and organic, but beyond that I just have to give examples.
		- A prime example of "aero" lines is the bodywork of an NIO EP9 (an electric hypercar).
		- Examples of completely organic for bodywork would be a Ferrari F50 and a Lamborghini Diablo. Examples of completely geometric bodywork are rare but Jeeps are good examples. The bodywork of newer Lamborghini's would count as geometric in the general sense. It's only straight lines and geometric means "only straight lines and constant radii arcs". But my type of geometric in "mechanical" specifically includes the arcs. So this isn't really the look I'm after.
		- Other examples of "aero"
			- wing tips on planes
			- ...
	- "Mechanical"
		- "Mechanical" is my name for the geometric lines found in mechanisms. Straight lines with fillets - section of a circle joining those lines.
		- "Engineering"/"industrial/"functional" look
		- Looks like it's been drawn on CAD
		- Mechanical parts like engines, gearboxes, the insides of watches, are prime examples.
		- Other examples
			- most cast metal parts
			- most machined metal parts
			- most industrial equipment
		- Curves with non-constant radii don't count and "mechanical" here even if they're completely functional e.g. a camshaft profile
		- Other examples of "mechnical"
	- ** For everyday items and things I'm not designing e.g. cooking utensils I'll be a bit flexible and say they have to have these lines "where possible".
