  - provisional_description:
  	- Opinions, task-questions, and maybe a few other types of notes/"ideas". Type of ideas that part of "train of thought". This is the main category of content on my PKM system.
  	- Contrasted with "map declarative notes" - in "statement-etc declarative notes", the notes basically correspond to a sentence each. Whereas in "map declarative notes", the notes correspond to "concepts", and sentences are formed by two notes and the relationship between them. E.g. for "life event decomposition", the "statement-etc declarative notes" include "break up time in proportion to importance of periods, not equal periods" and have ~4-6 periods on each level". And the "map declarative notes" include "1993-2001 - Scotland childhood years", "2001-2010 - Adelaide school years", "2011-2013 - natural personality development years", etc. The second set of notes are the periods that you get when you apply the principles from the first set of notes. The point in the "map declarative notes" is that it's easier to explain your conclusions with diagrams than sentences. But you need the "statement-etc declarative notes" for the chain of reasoning to the conclusions.
  	- Also contrasted with "non-content notes" - categories for grouping opinions, navigation points, etc.

  - edit 2026/03/26: Having algorithm-stage run from bottom to top to in navigation menus to match layout on (upcoming) graphs now. The upward layout is to give a sense of progress in life. The life timeline runs upward for this reason. That means chronological chain of thought runs upward. That mean Algorithm/argument generally runs upward. It doesn't always because when I form a false belief, I have to revise it, so I have to go back down before going upward. Also note that this only applies to single chains of reasoning. Once I've finished a chain (arrived at a decision), new chains start from below. It's not as if I have all the pattern recognition reasoning at the start of my life (when I'm a child) and only make decisions when I reach old age. But the chains of reasoning tend to follow chronological order more than any other direction. So it's good to position the argument stages going upward like this to make the whole system a bit more harmonious. (As well as give a bit of the "upward progress" feeling for when reasoning stage does track with time)
